# S5. The sampler

The model is sampled by particle Metropolis within Gibbs (Gunawan et al., 2020), as implemented in EMC2 and its development line DMC2 (Stevenson et al., 2026), extended with joint moves of the group and subject parameters. This section describes the sampler as implemented in DMC2 main **f2f58985**. That build contains every sampling mechanism used by any reported fit, with three exceptions, which are described at the end of the section (S5.7). Table S5.1 lists which mechanisms each build had.

The notation follows S4: n subjects, p parameters per subject, α_s the subject parameters, μ and Σ the population mean and covariance, a the Huang–Wand variables, ℓ_s the subject's log-likelihood. Citations are to files at f2f58985.

## S5.1 One iteration

Each iteration of each chain does three things in order (`src/sampler/runner.cpp`):

1. **Gibbs step** for (μ, Σ, a), using the conditionals of S4. μ is drawn given the current Σ; Σ given the new μ and the current a; then each a_k.
2. **Joint moves** (S5.3).
3. **Subject update**: a conditional particle step for every subject (S5.2).

Chains run in parallel. Each starts from μ⁽⁰⁾ ~ N(0, I) and Σ⁽⁰⁾ ~ IW(3p, I). Each subject's starting value is one of 1,000 draws from N(μ⁽⁰⁾, Σ⁽⁰⁾), picked with probability proportional to its likelihood.

## S5.2 Subject update: conditional importance resampling

For subject s with current value α_s, the step draws N candidate values θ₁, …, θ_N, keeps the current value as θ₀, and selects one of the N + 1 with probability proportional to a weight w_i. Because the current value is always among the candidates, this is a conditional (particle Gibbs) step that leaves the posterior invariant. The number of particles is

$$N_0 = \mathrm{round}(50\sqrt{p}),$$

doubled in preburn. It is adapted in the sample-kernel tuning (S5.4), within [25, 1.2 N₀].

**Proposal components.** The candidates come from a mixture of Gaussian components:

| component | centre | covariance | stages |
|---|---|---|---|
| group | μ | Σ | all |
| local | current α_s | preburn: ε²Σ; later: ε²(L_s + Σ⁻¹)⁻¹, or ε² × the chain covariance C_s | all |
| conditional | (L_s + Σ⁻¹)⁻¹(l_s + Σ⁻¹μ), or the chain mean | (L_s + Σ⁻¹)⁻¹, or C_s | burn, adapt, sample |
| efficient | the subject's conditional mean given the chain's group draw | its conditional covariance | sample |
| history (optional) | one Gaussian per chain, from its recent draws | its recent covariance | when `history_proposals = TRUE` |

In this table:

- **L_s and l_s** come from a quadratic approximation to the subject's log-likelihood, ℓ_s(x) ≈ c + l_sᵀx − ½xᵀL_s x. It is estimated within each chain at every burn, adapt and sample-kernel build:
  - by bracketed finite differences at the subject's mean draw, with negative curvature set to zero;
  - in directions where the draws are informative, refined by their empirical precision.
- **C_s** is the covariance of the subject's recent draws, pooled over chains (each chain contributes a random half of its recent window). The mean is likelihood-weighted.
- **The efficient component** fits, over the last 250 adapt iterations, a joint Gaussian to (α_s, μ, the log-Cholesky factor of Σ). It conditions α_s on one fixed draw of the group parameters per chain.

Default mixture weights:

- preburn: (.5, .5) over (group, local);
- burn and adapt: (.1, .4, .4) over (group, local, conditional);
- sample: .35 for the efficient component, and the adapted adapt weights scaled by .65 for the rest.

The weights adapt during burn and adapt towards components whose particles beat the current value more often: π ← normalise(max{.02, .95π + .05 · perf/Σperf}).

**Exact kernels (burn, adapt, sample).** Each update first decides, with probability equal to the local weight, whether to take a local or an independent step.

- **Local step.** Draw an auxiliary centre c ~ N(α_s, ε²K), then N particles from N(c, ε²K). Given c, the current value and the N particles are exchangeable draws from the same Gaussian, so the proposal density cancels:

  $$\log w_i = \log N(\theta_i;\mu,\Sigma) + \ell_s(\theta_i).$$

- **Independent step.** Draw the particles from the mixture of the non-local components, weights renormalised, and weight them by importance sampling:

  $$\log w_i = \log N(\theta_i;\mu,\Sigma) + \ell_s(\theta_i) - \log \sum_{k} \pi_k\, q_k(\theta_i),\qquad i = 0,\ldots,N .$$

Both steps leave the conditional posterior p(α_s | μ, Σ, data) invariant.

**Preburn** uses a search kernel instead: one mixture of all components, including the current-centred local one, inside the importance weights. That kernel is not a valid MCMC kernel. It is used only to find the region of high posterior density, and its draws are discarded.

**Scale adaptation.** In the exact kernels only the local scale ε adapts. The statistic r is the fraction of local particles whose weight beats the retained value's, and the target is 3%, where the local move's effective sample size per iteration peaks:

$$\varepsilon \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}_{[0.01,\,20]}\Big(\varepsilon\,\exp\{0.1\cdot\mathrm{clip}_{[-1,3]}((r - .03)/.03)\}\Big).$$

The initial ε is .5 (p > 15), .6 (p > 10) or .7. The other components keep scale 1.

## S5.3 Joint moves of (μ, α, Σ)

Each joint move proposes a common change to every subject and to the group parameters, and is accepted by Metropolis–Hastings using every subject's exact likelihood. They mix along directions where the subject-by-subject Gibbs steps are slow: a population mean shifting with all subjects, or a population SD shrinking with the subjects' spread.

They run in burn, adapt, the sample-kernel tuning and sample (`options(emc.scale_move = FALSE)` turns them off). Each iteration makes:

- p scale moves;
- p location moves and one coordinate move;
- one affine move and one spread move along a flow chosen at random.

**Scale move** (parameter j). Draw δ ~ N(0, σ²_step,j) and set c = e^δ. Then

- α_js → μ_j + c(α_js − μ_j) for every subject;
- Σ → DΣD with D = diag(1, …, c, …, 1);
- a_j → a_j/c²;
- μ is unchanged.

The subject and inverse-Wishart Jacobians cancel, leaving

$$\log r = \delta - (c^2 - 1)\frac{A_j^{-2}}{a_j} + \sum_s\big[\ell_s(\alpha_s') - \ell_s(\alpha_s)\big].$$

**Location move** (direction d_j). All subjects and μ shift together: α_s → α_s + δd_j, μ → μ + δd_j, with δ ~ N(0, σ²_loc,j). Every N(α_s; μ, Σ) is unchanged, so

$$\log r = -\delta\, d_j^\top\Sigma_0^{-1}(\mu - \mu_0) - \tfrac12\delta^2 d_j^\top\Sigma_0^{-1}d_j + \sum_s \Delta\ell_s .$$

The directions are prior-whitened eigenvectors of the summed curvature: with R = chol(Σ₀) and RᵀΣ_s L_s R + I = Q diag(λ) Qᵀ, d_j = R Q_j / √max(λ_j, 1). So stiff directions get short steps. They are rebuilt at each stage change and kept fixed within a stage.

The **coordinate move** is the same with d = a column of R and δ ~ N(0, 1), not adapted.

**Affine move.** Pick a generator G (an affine velocity field, see below), draw δ ~ N(0, σ²_aff), and set exp(δG) = [A b; 0 1]. Then α_s → Aα_s + b, μ → Aμ + b, Σ → AΣAᵀ. The subjects' normal densities cancel their Jacobian. The remaining Jacobian |A|^{p+2}, combined with the inverse-Wishart prior, gives

$$\log r = (2-\nu-p)\log|A| - \nu\sum_k\frac{(\Sigma'^{-1})_{kk} - (\Sigma^{-1})_{kk}}{a_k} - \tfrac12\Big[(\mu'-\mu_0)^\top\Sigma_0^{-1}(\mu'-\mu_0) - (\mu-\mu_0)^\top\Sigma_0^{-1}(\mu-\mu_0)\Big] + \sum_s\Delta\ell_s .$$

The generators are learned from the data:

- Each subject's score information is combined across subjects for a velocity field linear in the subject's position.
- The generators are the weakest directions of that information: the smallest eigenvector, or every whitened direction weaker than the group precision, when there are at least two.

**Spread move.** Along a flow with a translation part, stretch the subjects' projection on that flow about their centroid by e^δ, and move μ with the centroid. Σ is integrated out of the acceptance ratio and redrawn from its conditional on acceptance. With S = Σ_s(α_s − μ)(α_s − μ)ᵀ + 2ν diag(1/a):

$$\log r = (n-1)\delta - \tfrac12(\nu + p - 1 + n)\big[\log|S'| - \log|S|\big] - \tfrac12\Delta\big[(\mu-\mu_0)^\top\Sigma_0^{-1}(\mu-\mu_0)\big] + \sum_s\Delta\ell_s .$$

**Step sizes.** Every step size adapts towards acceptance .3, by σ ← σ e^{0.1(accepted − .3)}. They are frozen at the end of the sample-kernel tuning: the scale and location steps at their tail geometric mean, the affine and spread steps at their last value. Nothing adapts during sampling.

## S5.4 Stages and stop rules

| stage | what happens | ends when |
|---|---|---|
| preburn | search kernel, 2N particles, no joint moves | 50 iterations |
| burn | exact adapt kernel; proposals rebuilt every 100 iterations | the mean split-R̂ over all subject parameters and μ is < 1.1 |
| adapt | as burn; builds the efficient proposal | (i) the first parameter has at least 150 unique values for every subject and the efficient proposal can be built; then (ii) the largest subject R̂ over the last 250 iterations is < 1.2, or 1,000 adapt iterations, or no improvement over three checks |
| sample-kernel tuning | 100 iterations (labelled adapt) under the sample kernel, with tuning on | freezes ε (tail geometric mean), N (towards an ESS of 2.5√p), the history proposal and the joint-move steps |
| sample | frozen kernel; draws kept | the stop rule below, checked every 100 draws, with at least 1,000 draws per chain |

**Stop rule.** It is max(max_j R̂(μ_j), Q.99{R̂(α_sk)}) < 1.1. This is fit()'s `max_gd = 1.1, gd_quantile = .99, selection = c("alpha", "mu"), omit_mpsrf = TRUE`, applied with at most 40 sample checks for race models and 20 for diffusion models (`max_tries`).

If the rule fails on all of a stage's draws, it is recomputed with the first third dropped. That shorter history is kept only if every criterion passes on it.

**Split-R̂.** Each chain is cut in two halves of h draws, giving m = 2 × chains segments. With within-segment variance W and between-segment variance B/h,

$$\hat R = \sqrt{\frac{\frac{h-1}{h}W + B/h}{W}},$$

with no degrees-of-freedom correction and no transform. This is the split-R̂ of Vehtari et al. (2021) without their rank normalisation and folding. The adapt check alone uses the unsplit R̂.

**Singular covariance.** If a draw of Σ is numerically singular, the Gibbs step is redrawn from the unchanged state with fresh random numbers, up to three times. Then the fit stops with an error naming the parameters involved. There is no ridge and no carry-forward.

## S5.5 `hardfit()`

`hardfit()` in DMC2 main (used for `ANT_RDM_BcSi_hf1`) runs the same sampler with the campaign's rule and four additions:

- **R̂ selection:** includes the population variances (σ²).
- **Sample-stage criteria:**
  - the smallest ESS of μ and log population SDs is at least 100;
  - no chain's median summed log-likelihood is more than 100 below another's;
  - no subject's median log-likelihood differs by more than 15 between chains.
- **Chain replacement.** A chain is replaced when it is flagged at two consecutive checks. It is flagged when it sits more than 200 log-likelihood units and 5 within-chain MADs below the best chain, and is also lower on its log posterior unless the gap exceeds 1,000.
  - The replacement copies a random other chain's state from at least 100 draws before the end of its window, including its kernel settings.
  - At the end of burn every flagged chain is replaced, without the persistence requirement.
- **Early stop:** at sample check 20, if the rule's R̂ exceeds 1.5.

## S5.6 R̂ along the identified direction

`rhat_ident` (S6) standardises each μ series by its pooled within-chain SD. It projects the series on the eigenvector of the average within-chain covariance with the smallest eigenvalue, the best-identified direction, and computes the split-R̂ of the projection.

## S5.7 Mechanisms of the campaign builds not in f2f58985

1. **Flat test** (campaign waves 1–3 and the recovery studies; e8bf8344 `R/chain_diagnostics.R`). A sample-stage stop criterion, `max_flat_loc = 0.3`, required each monitored series to be flat between the first and last thirds of the stage. For a series x pooled over chains,

   $$\text{drift} = \frac{|\mathrm{median}(x_{\text{first third}}) - \mathrm{median}(x_{\text{last third}})|}{\mathrm{median}\{\mathrm{IQR}(x_{\text{first third}}), \mathrm{IQR}(x_{\text{last third}})\}} .$$

   The criterion is that drift ≤ 0.3 for:
   - every μ and the summed log-likelihood (their maximum);
   - the .99 quantile over subject parameters and over subjects' log-likelihoods.

   f2f58985 reports these drifts but does not stop on them.
2. **Thinning.** Campaign fits were thinned after sampling to ⌈2 × 3000/chains⌉ evenly spaced draws per chain (`thin_to_ess`, target ESS 3,000); the unthinned objects were kept but are not archived.
3. **Location move of the first joint-move build** (d0b6cf0b, the `jm` fits). Each parameter's location move shifted μ_j and every α_js along that coordinate. The step was adapted towards acceptance .3 and frozen at its tail geometric mean. Later builds move along the curvature directions of S5.3 instead, plus the prior-scale coordinate move.

The campaign's chain culling (`cull = TRUE` in fit(), burn and sample) is the chain replacement of S5.5 under earlier settings:

- **9e8727dd (waves 1–2, first DDMs):** a chain was flagged at a median log-likelihood gap of 10 and z > 2.5 (sample stage: z > 5 from wave 2); up to two replacements per stage; 100 iterations of re-burn after a sample-stage replacement. A single subject could also be replaced from another chain when it sat more than 5 units and 3 MADs below it (cell culling).
- **e8bf8344 (wave 3, the last DDMs, the recovery studies):** S5.5's chain thresholds (gap 200, z 5, log-posterior guard below a gap of 1,000, one replacement in sample), with cell culling removed. The opt-in sample criteria (ESS, LL gap, subject spread, early stop) are those of S5.5, at the values in `code/02_fit/fit_options.csv`.
