# S6. Convergence classes and which fits are displayed

## The stop rule

A fit converged if, over its sample-stage draws,

$$\max\Big(\max_j \hat R(\mu_j),\; Q_{.99}\{\hat R(\alpha_{ik})\}\Big) < 1.1,$$

with at least 1,000 sample draws per chain. Here R̂ is the split-R̂ (S5), the first term is the largest over the population means, and Q.99 is the .99 quantile over all subject parameters.

The population SDs are not part of the rule. With 518 subjects and up to 18 parameters, the largest of thousands of subject R̂ values exceeds 1.1 by chance alone. The .99 quantile asks that 99% of them be below it.

## Classes

Every model has several candidate runs (a jm refit, its continuations, campaign fits, Niek's refits). The model takes the class of its best run, in this order (`code/03_classify/display_index.R`):

1. **Converged**: met the stop rule, with no subject-mode caveat.
2. **Converged, subject-mode caveat**: met the stop rule, but either:
   - the run is split: a subject's chains disagree by more than 15 log-likelihood units, or two chains' log-likelihoods differ by more than 100;
   - or a subject sits more than 10 log-likelihood units below its best value in another run of the same model, or more than 20 below a model nested in it (the subject-mode screen, below).
3. **Ridge-only, shown as converged**: the stop rule was not met, but the population means' R̂ along the least-variable direction (R̂_ident, below) is ≤ 1.1, the summed log-likelihood drifts by at most 0.2 interquartile ranges between the first and last thirds of sampling, the rule's R̂ is < 1.5, and the run has no subject-mode caveat.
4. **Ridge-only, R̂ ≥ 1.5**: as class 3, but the rule's R̂ is at least 1.5.
5. **Not converged**: everything else.

**R̂_ident** is the R̂ of the population means projected on the pooled within-chain direction of least variance. It measures whether the chains agree on the identified part of the posterior, as opposed to drifting along a ridge of near-equivalent parameter combinations (`R/stop_stats.R` in e8bf8344 and later builds). A ridge-only fit's DIC is good to about ±250.

**Subject-mode screen** (T = 20 log-likelihood units, about 40 DIC). For each subject:

- compare its posterior-mean log-likelihood under a fit with its value under the same model's other runs, and under every model nested in it;
- a bigger model should fit each subject at least about as well as the models nested in it, so a subject far worse than a nested model marks the fit as stuck in a worse mode for that subject.

The restricted Simon LNR refits (S3) were screened only against fits inside the restricted model space (`derived/tables/screen_q6.csv`).

**Displayed as converged**: classes 1 and 3. Ridge-only models (class 3) are drawn with hatching and are never marked best:

- the zero point of each DIC page,
- the best box,
- the best two-mechanism marker,
- the model-type header value

all come from models that met the stop rule.

**RDM likelihood defect.** Every EMC2 build before 26 October 2026 had a C++ RDM density that, at extreme parameter values, returned a large positive log-likelihood where the correct value is about −2,100. One displayed fit was affected, `ANT_RDM_BcSi_jm`: three of its subjects sat in that region, gaining it about 1,560 DIC. A screen of all displayed fits compared each subject's log-likelihood at its posterior mean under the old and the corrected builds. Apart from those three subjects (+87 to +349), every subject of every fit agreed within 2.9 units. The affected model was refit with the corrected likelihood (`ANT_RDM_BcSi_hf1`, DMC2 main 1d56a061, `hardfit()`). Niek's refits were scored with EMC2 61e21587, which contains the fix.

Table S6.1 gives the counts by task and architecture, and `derived/tables/display_jm.csv` gives every model's class, run, rule R̂ and DIC.

# S7. Model selection

## Conditional DIC with the best-draw plug-in

The deviance information criterion (DIC; Spiegelhalter et al., 2002) is computed per subject and summed. For subject *i*, let ℓ_i(α) be the log-likelihood of its data, and let α_i^(m), m = 1, …, M, be its posterior draws. Define

- the mean deviance meanD_i = −2 · mean_m ℓ_i(α_i^(m));
- the deviance at the posterior mean Dmean_i = −2 ℓ_i(ᾱ_i);
- the best draw's deviance minD_i = −2 max_m ℓ_i(α_i^(m)).

Then

$$p_D = \sum_i \big[\mathrm{meanD}_i - \min(\mathrm{minD}_i, \mathrm{Dmean}_i)\big],\qquad \mathrm{DIC} = \sum_i \mathrm{meanD}_i + p_D .$$

This is EMC2's `compare(..., type = "conditional", use_best_fit = TRUE)`:

- It is *conditional* on the subject parameters: the group-level density is not part of the deviance.
- The plug-in deviance is the smaller of the deviance at the posterior mean and the best draw's, per subject.

Every DIC in the paper and in this archive is computed this way. DMC2 main from late October 2026 defaults to the standard DIC (pD = meanD − Dmean), so every call to its `compare()` here passes `use_best_fit = TRUE`.

**Why the best-draw plug-in.** In recovery study 5 (S9), the best-draw DIC beat the standard DIC (`code/08_recovery/conflict/dic_variants.R`, `recovery/conflict/dic_variants_rec5.csv`):

| DIC variant | one-mechanism data sets recovered | two-mechanism data sets recovered | largest margin of a wrong winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| best-draw plug-in (used) | 32 / 35 | 21 / 35 | 53 |
| standard | 12 / 35 | 19 / 35 | 8,961 |

The posterior mean of a strongly non-normal or multimodal subject posterior can sit in a region of poor fit, so the standard pD can be very large or even negative.

The joint DIC, which adds the group-level density, is not used; it failed to recover the generating models in earlier recovery studies.

**Matched draw counts.** A fit's DIC rises slowly with the number of stored draws, because the best draw improves and meanD stabilises. Comparisons between runs of the *same* model are therefore made at matched draw counts. Comparisons between models use each model's display run.

## The difference floor and the "=" rule

**DIC floor: 150.** This is the largest margin by which a wrong model beat the generating model in any of the recovery studies (149.4, LNR study), rounded up (`code/05_floors/recovery_floor.R`).

**"=" marks.** On the DIC grids, a model is marked "=" (not separable from its model type's best) when it is within:

- **50** of the best, if it has *fewer* mechanisms than the best. A wrong model with more mechanisms beat the generating one by at most 40 in studies 1–4 and 28 in study 5, so a simpler model within 50 of a more complex best is not rejected.
- **150** of the best, if it has as many or more mechanisms. This is the floor.

Two models are never marked "=" whatever their margin: Simon RDM Bi and Simon LBA Bi. Recovery study 5 rejects the one-mechanism model in those cells (S9).

A model type's header carries "(=)" when its best is within 150 of the page's best model. The full tables of the directional rule's consequences are `derived/tables/threshold_compare_{pairs,mechs}.csv` (`code/04_select/threshold_compare.R`).

## Delta and CAF misfit

For each displayed model, the misfit is computed from its subject-level posterior predictive (S8):

- **Delta misfit:** the RMS over the five quantiles of (median predicted delta − observed delta), as a percentage of the task's congruency effect (Simon 42 ms, Stroop 122 ms, ANT 70 ms).
- **CAF misfit:** the mean over congruency of the RMS over the five bins of (median predicted − observed accuracy), in percentage points.

**Difference floors.** Resample the participants with replacement 2,000 times, rebuild the observed group curve, and score it against the real one with the same formula. The floor is the 95th percentile (`code/05_floors/noise_floor_agg.R`).

| task | delta floor, mean curve (%) | delta floor, median curve (%) | CAF floor (% points) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simon | 7.52 | 15.57 | 0.49 |
| Stroop | 4.71 | 8.08 | 0.42 |
| ANT | 6.30 | 9.14 | 0.65 |

On the delta and CAF grids a model is marked "=" when its misfit is within the floor of its type's best, with no directional rule.

## Simple choice: WAIC, PSIS-LOO and weights

For the simple-choice task, the best fit of each family is also compared by WAIC and PSIS-LOO (Vehtari et al., 2017) on the trial-level log-likelihood, both on the deviance scale −2 × elpd (DMC2 main f2f58985; `code/04_select/compare_choice.R`).

- The relative efficiency for PSIS uses each subject's ESS.
- Observations with Pareto k > 0.7 are counted (`k_bad`).
- Model weights are pseudo-BMA+ (Bayesian bootstrap) and stacking (Yao et al., 2018).
- The choice recovery study (S9) recovered the generator with every criterion.

All three criteria rank the families in the same order, with the DDM first (Table S7.1). Stacking, which does not split weight between similar models, gives the DDM .61, the LBA .30 and the LNR .09.

# S8. Posterior predictives and figures

**Subject level only.** Every predictive in the paper and the archive is subject level (`code/06_predict/pp_subj.R`, `code/lib/predict.R`):

- 100 evenly spaced posterior draws, seed 42;
- each real participant is simulated from their own parameter draw on the model's own design, with the real trials (`make_data(shrink2bound = TRUE)`);
- no predictive is drawn from the population distribution.

**Statistics** (`stats_one()`):

- per participant, the .1, .3, .5, .7 and .9 quantiles of correct RTs by congruency (at least 5 trials);
- per participant, accuracy in five equal-count RT bins within congruency (at least 10 trials).

**Group curves:**

- the RT quantiles are aggregated over participants either as the mean (Vincentised curve) or as the median, which is robust to a few runaway simulated participants;
- accuracy is always the mean.

The heat grids and best5's model choice use the median curve. The delta function plots incongruent minus congruent quantile against their average.

**The figures** (`code/07_figures/`):

- **grid_dic / grid_delta / grid_caf** (`plot_heat_grid.R`):
  - every model of a task, as a tree per architecture: one-mechanism models at the root, adding a mechanism one level down;
  - colour gives the DIC difference from the page's best model, or the misfit, on a log scale offset by the difference floor and capped at the 98th percentile across the three pages;
  - cells are marked by class (S6) and by the "=" rule (S7).
- **convergence_grids** (`plot_lattice.R`): each model's class, laid out by the state of each parameter (absent, c, i, ci).
- **best5** (`plot_best5.R`):
  - delta (top) and CAF (bottom) for five models per task, drawn with EMC2's `plot_delta` and `plot_caf` from the subject-level predictives, so each draw's trials are pooled over participants;
  - LNR MiSi, RDM ViSi and LBA ViSi are fixed; the WDM and DDM columns show the best displayed model by the row's misfit (Stroop: WDM BcVc and DDM BcVc fixed).
- **models6** (`plot_models6.R`): RDM VciSci, ViSi, BciSci, BiSi and LNR MciSci, MiSi per task.
- **split6** (`plot_split6.R`): RDM ViSi for the 100 participants with the steepest and the 100 with the shallowest observed delta (or CAF) slope (mean of points 4–5 minus mean of points 1–2), each group drawn from its own participants' predictives.
- **choice** (`plot_choice.R`): the 16 simple-choice variants' DIC; defective CDFs for the five full variants; delta and CAF by stimulus for the DIC-best variant (DDM).
- **recovery_jm_fig, recovery_rec5_fig, choice_recovery_ic** (`code/08_recovery/`): S9.

# S9. Model-recovery studies

The recovery studies test whether conditional DIC selects the generating model, using data simulated to resemble the real data as closely as the fitted models allow.

**Simulation, in every study:**

- start from a fitted model of the real data (the generator);
- draw each real participant's parameters from MVN(posterior mean of μ, posterior mean of Σ);
- simulate that participant's real trials with `make_data(shrink2bound = TRUE)`;
- fit every candidate model to each simulated data set with the real fits' specifications (design, prior, chains) and the campaign's sampler settings.

Only the code, the design and the per-fit and per-set results are archived. The fits and simulated data sets are not.

**Conflict studies 1–4** (Simon; 5 replicates per generator; 340 fits; `code/08_recovery/conflict/studies1to4_specs.R`, `score_studies1to4.R`):

| study | generators | extra candidate | sets | recovered | largest wrong-winner margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 LNR | MiSi, MiSci, MciSi, MciSci | McSc | 20 | 10 | 149.4 |
| 2 RDM | ViSi, ViSci, VciSi, VciSci | BcSci | 20 | 6 | 81.6 |
| 3 LBA | ViSi, ViSci, VciSi, VciSci | BcSci | 20 | 7 | 86.8 |
| 4 cross-architecture | RDM ViSi, DDM BiVc | RDM VciSci, DDM BciSc | 10 | 10 | 0 |

Notes:

- Every LNR and RDM miss is a simpler model winning; the LBA has three over-complex winners, with margins of at most 40.
- In the cross-architecture study the generating architecture won every set:
  - by 955–1,288 over the best RDM on DDM-generated data;
  - by 2,105–2,424 over the best DDM on RDM-generated data;
  - by 29–124 over the nearest same-architecture model.

**Conflict study 5: one mechanism or two?** (`rec5_simulate.R`, `score_rec5.R`). Each cell pairs a one-mechanism model (G1) with the two-mechanism model that is its main rival on the real data, plus two other candidates:

- 7 cells, 2 generators, 5 replicates, 4 candidates: 280 fits;
- the one-mechanism generator was recovered in 32 of 35 sets;
- the wrong winners' margins were at most 28 on G1 data and 53 on G2 data.

Per cell (Table S9.1):

- false alarms: a two-mechanism model winning on G1 data;
- misses: G1 winning on G2 data;
- the real data's DIC difference between the best two-mechanism model and G1, against its simulated distribution under each generator.

**Choice recovery study** (`code/08_recovery/choice/`):

- generators: the DDM and LBA full simple-choice fits;
- 5 replicates each; candidates: the best variant of each of the five families; 50 fits.
- Every criterion (best-draw DIC, WAIC, PSIS-LOO) recovered the generator in all 10 sets.
- The DDM–LBA DIC difference was 648–834 on DDM data and −936 to −1,294 on LBA data. On the real data it is 147 in the DDM's favour.

**Reproduction.**

- *Exact:* the figures and tables reproduce from the archived fits, predictives and tables (`code/run_stage.R`).
- *Statistical:* a refit with `code/02_fit/fit.R` starts from the original seed, but not draw for draw. (Niek Stevenson's 60 refits are the exception: they were made with his own driver and are archived as fitted.) Parallel chains, a different BLAS, and the original runs' resumes after interruptions all change the random path.
