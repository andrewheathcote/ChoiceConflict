# S1. Data and design

**Source.** All four data sets are from the Self-Regulation Ontology study of Eisenberg et al. (2019), released under CC-BY 4.0: the simple choice reaction-time task, the Simon task, the Stroop task and the attention network task (ANT). The analysed data are in `inputs/data/<task>/dat.RData`, and `code/00_data/` rebuilds them from the published trial-level files.

**Exclusions.**

- **Simple choice** (`code/00_data/make_data.R`, from the original `parseChoice.R`):
  - practice trials removed;
  - participants with more than 25% omissions removed, then omitted trials;
  - participants with accuracy below .60 removed;
  - trials with RT below 0.2 s removed.

  This leaves 517 participants and 77,133 trials.
- **Conflict tasks** (`Data_Cleaning_Script.Rmd`). Participants are excluded for any of: median RT ≤ 200 ms; more than 25% omissions; accuracy ≤ 60%; one response key on more than 95% of trials. Trials are removed if RT < 0.15 s or RT exceeds the participant's mean plus three standard deviations, after which the omission criterion is checked again.

| task | participants | trials | factors (besides the response) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple choice | 517 | 77,133 | stimulus S (two levels) |
| Simon | 518 | 50,884 | relevant stimulus RS (colour), irrelevant stimulus IS (side) |
| Stroop | 520 | 48,956 | RS (ink colour), IS (word) |
| ANT | 518 | 73,193 | RS (target direction), IS (flanker direction), flanker location L (up, down), cue Q (no cue, alerting, orienting) |

In the ANT, neutral flankers are coded as congruent (IS = RS). The four cue types are reduced to three: centre and double cues both become "alerting".

**RT resolution.** Every likelihood is evaluated on RTs compressed to a resolution of 1/60 s (one frame at 60 Hz). At this resolution EMC2's compressed likelihood equals the uncompressed one for these data.

**Design factors.** These are defined once and used by every architecture. The functions that compute them are stored in each spec (`inputs/specs/`; listed in `derived/tables/specs_designs.csv`):

- CI: congruency, `RS == IS`, with levels (incong, cong). CIR has the reverse coding, levels (cong, incong).
- lR: the accumulator's response (latent response).
- lM: the accumulator matches the relevant stimulus, `RS == lR`.
- iM: the accumulator matches the irrelevant stimulus, `IS == lR`.

Two-level factors use difference contrasts: ADmat = (−½, ½)ᵀ, or its reverse ADmatR = (½, −½)ᵀ. A coefficient is therefore the difference between the two levels, with its sign fixed by the contrast.

# S2. Model likelihoods

Each race model has one accumulator per response. For a trial with response *r* and response time *t*, the likelihood is the defective density of the winning accumulator, times the survivor functions of the others, at the decision time *t* − *t*₀:

$$L(r, t) = f_r(t - t_0)\prod_{k \neq r} S_k(t - t_0), \qquad S_k = 1 - F_k .$$

The diffusion models (WDM, DDM) have a single accumulator with two boundaries.

Every trial's log-likelihood is bounded below at log(10⁻¹⁰). A trial whose parameters fall outside the model's bounds gets that floor value.

**Parameter transforms.** Each subject's parameters are design coefficients, sampled on an unbounded scale θ. They reach a trial in three steps:

1. The order restrictions of S3 replace a restricted coefficient by exp(θ).
2. The design matrix maps the coefficients to each cell's parameter.
3. The model transform maps that to the natural scale: the exponential for positive parameters, the normal CDF Φ for probability parameters, the identity for the LBA rate *v* and the LNR location *m*.

## Linear Ballistic Accumulator (LBA)

In the LBA (Brown & Heathcote, 2008), each accumulator *k* has:

- a start point uniform on [0, A];
- a drift rate drawn from N(v_k, sv_k²), truncated to positive rates;
- a threshold b_k = B_k + A.

All three of A, B and sv are on the exponential scale. With

$$z_1 = \frac{b - t v}{t\,sv}, \qquad z_2 = \frac{b - A - t v}{t\,sv},$$

the density and distribution function are

$$f(t) = \frac{1}{A\,\Phi(v/sv)}\Big[v\{\Phi(z_1) - \Phi(z_2)\} + sv\{\phi(z_2) - \phi(z_1)\}\Big],$$

$$F(t) = \frac{1}{\Phi(v/sv)}\Big[1 + \frac{b - A - tv}{A}\Phi(z_2) - \frac{b - tv}{A}\Phi(z_1) + \frac{t\,sv}{A}\{\phi(z_2) - \phi(z_1)\}\Big].$$

The A → 0 limit is used when A is fixed at 0. The denominator Φ(v/sv), from the truncation to positive rates, is bounded below at 10⁻¹⁰.

Identification: for the conflict tasks, sv is fixed at 1 for the base accumulator (`sv = log 1` on the sampled scale). The S mechanisms move sv away from 1 on the contrasts they carry.

## Racing Diffusion Model (RDM)

In the RDM (Tillman et al., 2020), each accumulator is a Wiener process with drift *v*, diffusion coefficient *s* and threshold *B*, starting at 0 (A = 0). Its first-passage time is Wald (inverse Gaussian). With k = B/s and l = v/s,

$$f(t) = \frac{k}{\sqrt{2\pi t^{3}}}\exp\!\Big\{-\frac{(k - l t)^2}{2t}\Big\},\qquad F(t) = \Phi\!\Big(\frac{l t - k}{\sqrt t}\Big) + e^{2 l k}\,\Phi\!\Big(-\frac{k + l t}{\sqrt t}\Big).$$

Details:

- v, B and s are on the exponential scale, and v ≥ 10⁻³.
- s = 1 for the base accumulator; the S mechanisms move it.
- The code evaluates *F* in a form that avoids overflow of e^{2lk} (`src/wald_functions.h`).

Builds before 26 October 2026 had a defect in this density at extreme parameter values. Every displayed fit was screened against the corrected likelihood; one fit was affected and was refit (S6).

## Lognormal Race (LNR)

In the LNR (Heathcote & Love, 2012), the finishing time of accumulator *k* is lognormal, log(t − t₀) ~ N(m_k, s_k²). So

$$f(t) = \frac{1}{t\,s\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\Big\{-\frac{(\log t - m)^2}{2 s^2}\Big\},\qquad F(t) = \Phi\!\Big(\frac{\log t - m}{s}\Big).$$

Here m is on the identity scale and s on the exponential scale.

## Diffusion decision model (DDM) and Wiener diffusion model (WDM)

The parameters of the DDM (Ratcliff, 1978) are:

- boundary separation a;
- relative start point Z ∈ (0, 1), with absolute start z = Z·a;
- drift rate v and diffusion coefficient s (a, v and the variabilities are divided by s);
- across-trial drift SD sv;
- start-point range sz = 2·SZ·min(Z, 1 − Z)·a, so the range stays inside (0, a);
- non-decision time t₀ with uniform variability over [t₀, t₀ + st₀].

Transforms: a, sv, t₀, st₀ and s on the exponential scale; Z and SZ by Φ.

Densities:

- The first-passage density at the lower boundary is the series of Navarro and Fuss (2009), with sv integrated analytically:

  $$f_-(t \mid v, a, z, sv) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + sv^2 t}}\exp\!\Big\{\frac{sv^2 z^2 - 2vz - v^2 t}{2(1 + sv^2 t)}\Big\}\, f_0(t \mid a, z).$$

  Here f₀ is the zero-drift density, evaluated with the small- or large-time series as accuracy requires.
- The upper boundary uses −v and a − z.
- When sz or st₀ is not zero, the density is integrated over them numerically, by adaptive cubature with tolerance 5·10⁻³ (`src/model_DDM.cpp`).

The **WDM** is the DDM with sv = sz = st₀ = 0.

**Response coding.** For Simon and ANT, the response boundaries are the two responses, and the rate's sign follows the stimulus (`v ~ CI*RS`, with RS coded −1/+1). Stroop's diffusion models are fitted to accuracy-coded data (upper boundary = correct). So they have no start-point mechanism, and their congruency effect is on the rate towards the correct boundary.

# S3. Conflict parameterisations

## Mechanisms

A model is named by its architecture and the conflict mechanisms it contains, e.g. `RDM_ViSi` or `LNR_MciSci` (`code/lib/name_scheme.R`). A mechanism is a parameter plus the contrast it acts on:

- **c**, congruency: the parameter differs between congruent and incongruent trials, equally for both accumulators (factor CI or CIR).
- **i**, irrelevant match: the parameter differs between the accumulator that matches the irrelevant attribute and the one that does not (factor iM).
- **ci**: both contrasts on that parameter.

| architecture | parameter letters | mechanisms |
|---|---|---|
| RDM, LBA | B threshold, V rate, S rate variability (RDM s, LBA sv) | Bc, Bi, Vc, Vi, Sc, Si |
| LNR | M location m, S scale s | Mc, Mi, Sc, Si |
| WDM, DDM | B boundary a (Bc), B start point Z (Bi), V rate, S diffusion coefficient s, SV rate variability sv | Bc, Bi, Vc, Sc, SVc |

For the diffusion models, the start point is written as the i contrast on the threshold, because a start-point shift is an asymmetry between the two boundaries. That puts the diffusion models' names on the same footing as the race models'.

**Formulas.** Each architecture's full model has the terms below; a model with fewer mechanisms drops the corresponding terms. The base terms (response bias on B, the match effect on v and on the variability, and t₀) are in every model.

| architecture | full design |
|---|---|
| RDM | B ~ CIR + lR + iM; v ~ CI + lM + iM; s ~ CIR + lM + iM; t₀ ~ 1 (ANT: t₀ ~ L + Q); A = 0 |
| LBA | B ~ CIR + lR + iM; v ~ CI + lM + iM; sv ~ CIR + lM + iM; A ~ 1; t₀ as RDM |
| LNR | m ~ CI + lM + iM + lR; s ~ CI + lM + iM; t₀ as RDM |
| WDM / DDM | v ~ CI·RS (Simon, ANT) or v ~ CI (Stroop, accuracy coded); a ~ CI; Z ~ lb with the CI contrast; s ~ CI; sv ~ CI (DDM); t₀ as RDM |

Contrasts:

- **Race models:** ADmat for lR on B and for lM and iM on v; ADmatR for iM on B, and for lM and iM on the variability.
- **Diffusion models:** RS uses (−1, 1); the start point Z uses a contrast that shifts it on congruent trials only.

The full contrast matrices are stored in each spec.

**Which models were fitted.** For each task and architecture, the set was:

- every one- and two-mechanism model;
- every three- and four-mechanism model with at most one mechanism carrying only one of its c / i contrasts.

WDM/DDM models with an sv mechanism were fitted but are not drawn in the grids. In total there are 353 conflict-task models (ANT 119, Simon 132, Stroop 102), listed in `derived/tables/display_jm.csv`.

## Order restrictions

Several conflict effects are estimated as order restrictions: the coefficient is the exponential of an unbounded parameter, so it can only take the sign the theory predicts (`pre_transform = exp`).

- A restriction removes the region of parameter space the theory excludes. It therefore reduces the model's flexibility and cannot improve its fit.
- A restricted model is the more faithful statement of a mechanism whose direction the theory specifies. A mechanism that raises caution, or speeds the response favoured by the irrelevant attribute, is not agnostic about the sign of its effect.

| architecture | restricted (exp) | direction | in the stored specs |
|---|---|---|---|
| RDM, LBA | B_iMd | lower threshold for the accumulator matching the irrelevant attribute | every model with Bi |
| RDM, LBA | v_iMd | higher rate for the accumulator matching the irrelevant attribute | every model with Vi |
| RDM, LBA | v_CIcong | higher rate on congruent trials | every model with Vc |
| LNR | m_iMd | earlier finishing for the accumulator matching the irrelevant attribute | every model with Mi |
| LNR | m_CIincong | later finishing on incongruent trials | every model with Mc (Simon: the Q6 refits, below) |
| WDM, DDM (Simon, ANT) | v_CIcong:RSd | faster drift towards the correct boundary on congruent trials | every model with Vc |
| WDM, DDM (Simon, ANT) | Z_lbCI | on congruent trials, start point shifted towards the boundary of the response both attributes favour | every model with Bi |

Not restricted:

- B_CIRincong (Bc in the race models);
- the variability terms of the S mechanisms (Sc, Si, and SVc);
- LNR s_CIincong;
- the diffusion models' a_CIcong and s_CIcong;
- Stroop's accuracy-coded diffusion v_CIcong.

For each of these, either the theory does not fix a direction, or (Stroop diffusion) the effect is estimated freely. Every Stroop WDM/DDM posterior of v_CIcong lies in the predicted (positive) direction (Table S3.1).

**Simon LNR refits (Q6).** The original Simon LNR fits estimated m_CIincong freely. In two of them (Mci and MciSc) the estimate went the wrong way: incongruent trials finished earlier, with more than 98% of participants' posteriors confidently negative. In these models the restricted m_iMd term carried the conflict effect.

All eight Simon LNR models with an Mc mechanism were refitted with the restriction (02_fit sets `jmr` and `jmh1r`; 2026-11-07), and the paper reports the restricted fits. Effects of the restriction:

- it cost MciSc 338 DIC (599 behind the best Simon LNR model, MiSi);
- the other seven changed by between −30 and +31, well inside the DIC floor;
- the best Simon LNR model is unchanged, because MiSi has no congruency term on m.

# S4. Hierarchical structure and priors

Each participant *i* = 1, …, *n* has a vector of p parameters α_i on the sampled (unbounded) scale. The population model is multivariate normal with a full covariance matrix:

$$\alpha_i \mid \mu, \Sigma \sim N_p(\mu, \Sigma), \qquad i = 1, \ldots, n.$$

**Priors** (EMC2's defaults for the standard group type; no fit in this project overrides them, and `code/lib/transplant.R` stops if a fit's stored prior differs):

$$\mu \sim N_p(\mu_0 = 0,\; \Sigma_0 = I_p),$$

$$\Sigma \mid a_1, \ldots, a_p \sim IW\!\big(\nu + p - 1,\; 2\nu\,\mathrm{diag}(1/a_1, \ldots, 1/a_p)\big),\qquad a_k \sim IG\!\big(\tfrac12,\; 1/A_k^2\big),$$

with ν = 2 and A_k = 0.3 for every parameter.

This is the prior of Huang and Wand (2013). It implies half-t(ν) priors on the population standard deviations, with scale A_k, and marginally uniform priors on the correlations.

**Gibbs conditionals.** Given the subject parameters α₁, …, α_n:

$$\mu \mid \alpha, \Sigma \sim N_p\big(V(\Sigma_0^{-1}\mu_0 + n\Sigma^{-1}\bar\alpha),\, V\big), \qquad V = (\Sigma_0^{-1} + n\Sigma^{-1})^{-1},$$

$$\Sigma \mid \alpha, \mu, a \sim IW\!\Big(\nu + p - 1 + n,\; \sum_i(\alpha_i - \mu)(\alpha_i - \mu)^\top + 2\nu\,\mathrm{diag}(1/a)\Big),$$

$$a_k \mid \Sigma \sim IG\!\Big(\frac{\nu + p}{2},\; \nu\,(\Sigma^{-1})_{kk} + \frac{1}{A_k^2}\Big).$$

The inverse-Wishart draw uses the Bartlett decomposition. If a draw of Σ is numerically singular, the Gibbs step is redrawn, at most three times. No ridge is added and no previous value is carried forward, because either would change the model.

The subject parameters α_i are updated by the particle Metropolis-within-Gibbs step of section S5. They are also updated, jointly with μ and Σ, by the joint moves.

The order restrictions act inside the subject's likelihood. The prior is on the unbounded θ = α_i, so a restricted coefficient exp(θ) has a lognormal prior marginally.
